PROJECT 01

Designing an Annual Performance Appraisal System from the ground up

A 50-person financial services company · HR Operations Intern · Full-cycle system design, FY 2026–27

DESIGNED & IMPLEMENTED
END-TO-END

The brief

The company had no structured performance review process — increments and promotion decisions were made informally, with no consistent way to recognise contribution, track growth, or link individual performance to business objectives. I was asked to design a complete Performance Appraisal (PA) system from scratch: the review methodology, the assessment forms, the scoring model, the timeline, and the increment and improvement framework that would sit behind it.

The result is a documented, repeatable annual cycle now used as the reference for every employee, manager and HR stakeholder in the organisation.

A 360° feedback model

Rather than a single top-down manager rating, I built the system around 360° feedback — combining a self-assessment, the direct manager's review, and two independent peer reviews (one from within the employee's team, one from outside it). This reduces individual bias, surfaces strengths a manager alone might miss, and builds a culture of transparent, development-focused feedback. Peer identities are kept confidential throughout.

Assessment framework & weightage

Every employee is scored (1–5 scale) across four weighted sections, each scored by a different combination of reviewers depending on what it measures:

Professionalism30%

Growth10%

PROJECT 02

Building a Rewards & Recognition Program tied to core values

Same 50-person organisation · HR Operations Intern · Program design & policy documentation, effective Sept 2026

DESIGNED
FROM SCRATCH

The brief

Recognition at the company was informal and inconsistent — good work went unnoticed as often as it was celebrated, and there was no shared language for what "great performance" actually looked like. I designed a complete Rewards & Recognition Program that ties every award to a defined core value, scores nominees against explicit criteria, and runs on a repeatable quarterly/monthly/annual cycle — replacing ad hoc appreciation with a structured, fair, and visible system.

Five core values, made scoreable

Rather than leave "values" as a poster on the wall, I broke each one into concrete, observable parameters that a manager can actually score a nominee against — so recognition is evidence-based, not a popularity contest.

Consultative Mindset
Understands the ask, asks the right questions, offers thoughtful solutions
Client First Attitude
Puts client needs and experience at the centre of every decision
Ownership
Sees work through start to finish without waiting to be chased
Proactiveness
Anticipates needs and acts before being told to
Leadership Skills
Influences and supports others, creates clarity, lifts team results

Four quarterly Core Value Awards form the backbone of the program, layered with a monthly and an annual flagship award, plus automatic milestone awards for tenure.

Nomination & selection process

Nomination
One employee nominated per department by their AVP, scored against the award's specific criteria on the nomination form. Self-nominations are not accepted.
HR consolidation
HR compiles nominations and scores from both departments ahead of the recognition event.
Management selection
Leadership reviews the scored entries and selects the winner based on the strongest, most representative nomination.
Announcement & recognition
Winners are announced at the recognition event and receive a certificate, public recognition, and a gift.

Eligibility guardrails

To keep the program credible, every award carries the same baseline conditions: a minimum tenure (3 months for quarterly/monthly awards, 12 months for the annual award), no self-nominations, and exclusion for anyone serving a notice period.

The program is deliberately linked to the Performance Appraisal System (Project 01): an employee on an active Performance Improvement Plan or with an unresolved disciplinary matter is not eligible for nomination. Recognition and performance management were designed to reinforce each other, not run as two disconnected systems.

PROJECT 04

Turning HR data into dashboards that support decisions

Same 50-person organisation · HR Operations Intern · Attendance, leave, capacity, attrition, reporting and recruitment analytics

DESIGNED & BUILT
END-TO-END

The brief

HR data was spread across daily biometric exports, monthly leave files and an employee master sheet, with no single view that HR or management could read quickly. I designed a connected set of dashboards and trackers that turn those raw files into clear, policy-linked views of punctuality, leave, capacity, headcount, attrition and hiring.

The focus throughout was reusable infrastructure rather than one-off reports. Each dashboard is built on formulas and fixed structures so it can be refreshed with new data instead of rebuilt, and every one is designed to be read by a non-technical manager. Below, each dashboard is shown with what went in, how it works and what it told us.

1. Attendance and punctuality dashboard

The most detailed dashboard in the set. It gives HR and management a repeatable view of punctuality, working hours and presence for every employee, and flags policy violations early.

Inputs
Daily biometric attendance reports for every employee (in time, out time, work duration, late by, early going by, status), plus the rules of the Be On Time policy: shift hours, grace periods, late and early thresholds and escalation steps.
Analysis & logic
Per employee counts of late arrivals, early departures, incomplete 9-hour days and work after 7 PM, with the exact dates listed. Days present count as 1, half days as 0.5. Late flags are mutually exclusive, are measured against each person's own shift start, and are waived when the full 9-hour shift is completed.
Insights & decisions
Red, amber and green status levels show who needs attention first. More than three violations in any category is a red flag and moves into the policy's escalation steps. Attendance also feeds the Professionalism section of the appraisal system in Project 01.
CRITICAL
Red flag. More than three violations in a tracked category, escalated under the policy.
WARNING AND CAUTION
Amber levels that show a pattern building before it reaches the red flag limit.
OK
Within policy limits. No action needed.
SPECIAL CASES
Employees on notice are tracked but never red flagged. Different shift patterns are assessed against their own thresholds.

2. Leave tracking and utilisation

A leave tracker and utilisation view that shows how much leave is taken, of what type, and how it affects available capacity.

Inputs
Monthly leave files covering Privilege, Casual and Sick Leave, Sick and Planned WFH, Marriage and Bereavement Leave and Leave Without Pay.
Analysis & logic
Full days count as 1.0 and half days as 0.5, totalled per employee per leave type. Clean-up rules map medical leave to Sick Leave, skip public holidays, treat generic WFH as Planned WFH and split comma-separated entries. Leave utilisation is shown as overall capacity used, with employees grouped into bands by individual capacity.
Insights & decisions
Overall capacity used came out at about 90 percent, with 11 employees at full individual capacity and the rest spread across lower bands. Employees with zero leave were hidden so attention goes where leave actually occurs, and leave patterns were read alongside attendance. A department leave tracker with a daily calendar shows who is on leave, on WFH and how many people are available.

3. Workplace capacity management workbook

A weekly view of how much of the expected working time each employee actually spent in the office, built to support capacity planning.

Inputs
Monthly attendance dashboard files with one sheet per working day, and the employee roster matched by employee code to avoid name errors.
Analysis & logic
Daily office hours are totalled by week. Capacity percentage is hours in office divided by total working hours, counted only on days with a punch record so new joiners and leavers are not understated. Each week carries a factual note on absences and half days, plus a total utilisation row.
Insights & decisions
Colour bands make low and healthy utilisation visible at a glance. After a first version overstated working hours, the logic was corrected and each month was rebuilt from the original file rather than patched. The same thinking went into a 14-page Capacity Management Plan for management.
Below 40%40 to 70%Above 70%

4. Workforce, headcount and attrition dashboard

A Power BI dashboard built on the employee master sheet, giving leadership a live picture of who is in the organisation, who has left and why.

Inputs
The employee master sheet: joining and last working dates, department, location, gender, exit reasons, performance review status and open requisitions.
Analysis & logic
Headcount, gender and location splits, exits by reason and by department, a monthly attrition trend, review status and open positions. Attrition is calculated on average headcount. Tenure is shown as years and months, and a combination chart pairs exit counts with attrition percentage on a secondary axis.
Insights & decisions
Education was the largest single exit reason, which separated planned departures from performance or role-fit exits. Two departments accounted for most exits, so retention attention could be focused there. The open positions view gave leadership one list of roles, seats, budget and location.

5. Monthly HR tracker

A monthly scorecard of six metrics: employee capacity, attrition rate, new joinee rate, open positions, major events and red flags.

Inputs
A defined data set for each of six metrics: headcount and budget, exits and reasons, new hires and sources, requisitions and their age, the HR calendar and patterns across the other metrics.
Analysis & logic
Exits in the month divided by average headcount gives the attrition rate, and new hires divided by average headcount gives the new joinee rate. Open positions are tracked by age and pipeline stage. Major events and red flags are qualitative and drawn from the HR calendar.
Insights & decisions
Metric cards, six-month trend charts, an events list and a red flag list that can be marked resolved. Triggers such as a spike in exits or a role open for more than 60 days turn trends into a visible action list for HR.

6. HR reporting and analytics workbook (MIS)

A reusable Excel workbook for the full April to March year, covering the two departments. It was delivered as structure and formulas, ready to take data.

Inputs
Eight structured input sheets: Employee Master, Attendance, Recruitment, Employee Movements, Salary, Market Salary, Capacity and Workforce, and an HR Incident Log, plus a Lists sheet for dropdowns.
Analysis & logic
Reports cover hiring and recruitment, attrition and movements, workforce capacity and forecast, attendance, salary against market, diversity and major HR incidents. Six configurable period columns allow weekly, monthly, quarterly and half-yearly comparison, using SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, AVERAGEIFS and SUMPRODUCT so it works in any Excel version without refreshing.
Insights & decisions
A single reporting structure for HR to run every period instead of rebuilding reports each time. Shared rules such as attrition on average headcount and mutually exclusive late flags keep numbers consistent across reports, and a reference bug caused by header rows was found and fixed during testing.

7. Recruitment, training and candidate trackers

Supporting trackers that define hiring metrics and keep candidates and training activity organised.

Inputs
Pipeline dates and offer data, an existing candidate template, and the training activities planned for each week.
Analysis & logic
Time to Hire (pipeline entry to offer acceptance, distinguished from Time to Fill), Quality of Hire, and Offer Acceptance Rate (offers accepted divided by offers made), each with a formula, worked example and benchmark. The candidate tracker uses dropdowns for status, CV source and notice period, with filters and frozen panes, plus an Interview sheet.
Insights & decisions
Every candidate's stage and interview feedback sits in one place, and recruitment metrics can be read against benchmarks. The training tracker groups onsite and online training, preparation, discussions, hiring and live projects by week, with a comments column for review.

Connecting the dashboards

The dashboards were designed to work together. An individual employee template brings each person's attendance, leave and policy adherence into one record, using XLOOKUP and COUNTIF so data updates without manual copying. The same attendance logic is reused across the attendance dashboard, the capacity workbook and the MIS workbook, so one rule gives the same answer everywhere.

These analytics link back to the other projects: attendance data supports the Professionalism score in the Performance Appraisal System (Project 01), and dashboards and web parts were deployed on the intranet (Project 03). A guide for sending biometric attendance data into the HR system was also written, so attendance can feed performance management directly.

PROJECT 06

Handling recruitment from job description to interview

Same 50-person organisation · HR Operations Intern · Job descriptions, sourcing, screening and candidate tracking

HANDLED
HIRING TO INTERVIEW

The brief

As the company grew, new roles opened across its teams and I took on the hiring process alongside my other HR work. I wrote the job descriptions and vacancy posts, found and approached candidates through job portals, professional networks and agencies, managed applications in the applicant tracking system and held the first round of telephonic interviews. Candidates were successfully placed into the organisation.

The hiring process I ran

Define the role
Turn the manager's requirements into a clear job description, using an existing company sample so every JD has the same structure and tone.
Advertise and source
Publish vacancy posts, search for candidates on Naukri and LinkedIn Recruiter, share openings with employees for referrals and prepare a list of hiring agencies for financial services roles.
Screen and interview
Review applications in the applicant tracking system and hold telephonic interviews to shortlist candidates for the managers.
Track and measure
Keep every candidate's stage and interview feedback in one tracker, and read hiring speed and offer acceptance against benchmarks (see Project 04).

What I produced

Job descriptions
Wrote JDs for the Founders' Office Intern role and the full-time Analyst for the expert platform, matching one standard structure. I rewrote the responsibilities into short one-line points in a student-friendly tone, because candidates tend to skip long lists.
Vacancy posts and announcements
Drafted vacancy posts with the role, qualifications and skills needed, and adapted one hiring message for three open roles in the FRA team into email and text formats for sharing with employees and their networks.
Sourcing channels
Used Naukri and LinkedIn Recruiter, registered the company on the ICAI portal to reach potential candidates, and listed hiring agencies in Pune for financial services professionals in Excel.
Employee referrals
Created an employee referral email template that announces a role, explains the incentive and sets out how to submit a referral, linked to the referral policy in Project 05.
The candidate tracker, the interview sheet and the recruitment metrics (Time to Hire, Quality of Hire and Offer Acceptance Rate) are covered in Project 04. Offer letters, appointment letters and onboarding follow in the employee lifecycle portfolio.
